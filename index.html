01   /   Current research

Theoretical physics at the attosecond scale.

My doctoral research is in theoretical atomic, molecular, optical, and condensed matter physics, with an emphasis on open quantum systems and attosecond light–matter interactions.

I am interested in how quantum systems evolve under ultrafast excitation, how their surroundings affect that evolution, and how a mathematical description can reveal the physical processes underneath an observation.

Before turning to theory, I spent several years working with real materials and devices. That experience continues to shape how I approach a physical question.

05   /   A little background

A physicist by curiosity, an engineer by experience.

I am a Physics Ph.D. student at the University of Arizona. I came to theoretical physics by a somewhat circuitous route: an undergraduate education in electrical engineering, followed by a master's in applied physics and engineering at Northeastern University.

Along the way, I worked with magnetic materials, two-dimensional semiconductors, piezoelectric MEMS and their experimental apparatus. Each project offered a different way to ask how nature works, and a different appreciation for the distance between a clean model and an actual measurement.

Today, my attention is on theory. I still find the most interesting questions to be those that can be approached with mathematical precision and a strong sense of physical reality.
